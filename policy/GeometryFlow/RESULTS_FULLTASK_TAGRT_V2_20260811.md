# Full-task TAGRT v2 status — 2026-08-11

## Scope and frozen method line

This experiment keeps the paper line fixed as a learned, geometry-conditioned
manipulation policy.  It does not introduce a planning residual, a hand-written
stage gate, or an oracle pose at deployment.

The v2 policy consumes a three-frame memory containing scene/object point
tokens, NDF/TAGRT local anchor-relation tokens, a global remaining-SE(3) token,
and robot EEF/gripper state.  Future action queries cross-attend to the complete
memory.  The output is a six-step bimanual action chunk with a continuous arm
head and a separate binary gripper head.  Both direct regression and conditional
flow-matching action decoders use the same observation backbone.

## Dataset and strict split

- Source: 50 successful `place_shoe_geometry_marker` demonstrations.
- Prepared archive: 4,440 samples with three-frame history and stride 2.
- Train shoe IDs: 2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9.
- Validation shoe IDs: 1, 6.
- Test shoe IDs: 0, 5.
- The geometry source is camera point cloud/NDF output; simulator pose is not a
  deployment input.

## Teacher-forced held-object results

Across seeds 0/1/2, direct-regression TAGRT reduced normalized test motion MSE
from `1.155388 +/- 0.138177` (raw-point Transformer) to
`0.963399 +/- 0.034677`, an average relative improvement of `15.898%`.

Across seeds 0/1/2, flow-matching TAGRT reduced normalized test motion MSE from
`1.266895 +/- 0.085083` to `1.070442 +/- 0.118413`, an average relative
improvement of `15.673%`.

Shuffling the local relation tokens across goals and zeroing geometry both hurt
the result in every tested seed.  This is evidence that the policy uses the
task-aligned geometry rather than merely benefiting from more parameters.

Interpretation: this closes the offline representation/usefulness question, but
it does not close online task success.

## Capacity and one-episode admission diagnostics

The initial 0.756M-parameter 2x2 pilot was under-capacity for exact sequence
memorization.  A 5,656,526-parameter, four-memory/four-decoder-layer model with
dropout disabled overfit episode 2 to:

- normalized motion MSE: `0.00101798`;
- translation MAE: `0.0096075 cm` (about `0.096 mm`);
- rotation MAE: `0.01470 deg`;
- gripper accuracy: `0.998996`.

A 1 mm training-only open-gripper EEF jitter reduced synthetic first-action
translation MAE from `0.152 cm` to `0.104 cm` and RMSE from `0.294 cm` to
`0.176 cm`.  A 10 mm jitter was too large and substantially damaged fitting.

Interpretation: model capacity and mild state-noise augmentation matter, but
near-zero teacher-forced error is not sufficient for online success.

## Online action-contract admission test

An evaluation-index bug was found: the earlier "episode 2 replay" evaluations
initialized `now_ep_num=0`.  Those learned-policy replay failures are therefore
not valid exact-replay controls.  `script/eval_policy.py` now accepts and records
`evaluation_start_episode_index`, and the corrected replay uses episode index 2.

The more important result is that the corrected expert-action control still
fails:

1. Replaying 83 stride-2 expert EEF targets through `take_action(...,
   action_type="ee")`: 0/1 success and no shoe motion.
2. Replaying all 166 next-frame expert EEF poses: 0/1 success and no shoe
   motion, even though those poses match the HDF5 next-frame EEF states to
   numerical precision (`5.94e-9` mean absolute difference).
3. Replaying all 166 sparsely recorded joint drive targets through
   `take_action(..., action_type="qpos")`: 0/1 success and no shoe motion.

This invalidates the current online comparison protocol.  Demonstrations were
generated by high-level primitives that create dense planner joint trajectories.
The HDF5 `endpose` values are observed states along those trajectories.  Passing
the next observed EEF state back into `take_action(..., "ee")` invokes a new
motion-planning/control trajectory, so it is not a replay of the command that
generated the demonstration.  Contact and gripper timing therefore need not be
reproduced.

The corrective admission experiment records every low-level simulator command
before its physics step: 14 joint/gripper drive targets and 12 arm velocity
targets.  Episode 0 contains 2,214 such commands and 165 camera observations
with a monotonic observation-to-command index.  Replaying those commands
directly, without IK or TOPP replanning, succeeded after a fresh reset of the
same seed (`1/1`, full grasp and placement).  It reached the raw pose criterion
at policy chunk 119 and the required 25-step hold at chunk 126.  This confirms
that the previous failure came from action conversion/replanning, not from an
ungraspable scene.

## Current claim boundary

Supported now:

- relation-preserving TAGRT improves held-object action prediction over a
  capacity-matched raw-point Transformer on a strict unseen-shoe split;
- the benefit is stable over three training seeds and survives both regression
  and flow-matching decoders;
- shuffled/zero interventions show causal dependence on the geometry tokens;
- the intended Transformer has enough capacity to memorize the demonstration
  action sequence accurately.

Not supported yet:

- successful full-task online execution;
- an online success-rate advantage over raw Transformer or DP3;
- a real-camera deployment claim.

The current online 0% result must not be used to reject NDF, TAGRT, or the policy
architecture because the expert action replay itself fails the same interface.

## Required gate before more policy training

1. Define labels in the same control space consumed online.  Prefer the recorded
   dense joint target/control trajectory, or explicitly log commanded absolute
   EEF targets during collection; do not use differences between observed EEF
   states as if they were commands.
2. Rebuild the full-task archive at the original control cadence around grasp and
   release.  Do not apply stride-2 subsampling until replay equivalence is shown.
3. Require expert-command replay to reproduce the task (target: 10/10) before
   admitting learned-policy online results.
4. Only after that gate, train the same large raw and TAGRT Transformers and run
   paired online evaluation on identical seeds.  Flow matching remains a decoder
   ablation, not the method's central claim.

Implementation status: the dataset/controller now support a replay-equivalent
15-step `dense_joint26` action block (left position/velocity/gripper and right
position/velocity/gripper).  The Transformer decoder was generalized from 12
to 24 continuous channels while keeping the independent two-channel gripper
head.  Exact dataset alignment, a one-epoch CPU smoke train, and runtime
inference have passed.  The next admission gate is 10/10 dense expert replay,
followed by one-episode learned overfit through this same controller.

This keeps the paper story unchanged: task-aligned local relations and global
remaining geometry are continuous conditions for a learned manipulation policy.
The immediate blocker is the dataset-to-controller action contract, not a need
to return to planning or hand-written stages.
