\section{Discussion and Limitations}

Our method relies on category-level PartNext annotations and a consistent part taxonomy, and currently learns a separate semantic field for each object family. It therefore does not provide open-vocabulary or category-free transfer, while its annotation and training costs grow with the number of object categories. Discrete canonical part labels are also less suitable for deformable objects and ambiguous, continuous, or task-dependent functional regions. At deployment, the field conditions on segmented partial object clouds and does not require observing the complete object surface. Nevertheless, its predictions may degrade under severe occlusion, complete invisibility of a functional part, sparse or corrupted depth, and segmentation failure. Finally, the representation captures part-aware geometric and semantic structure but does not explicitly model physical properties, object dynamics, or task-conditioned affordances. Future directions include shared fields, weaker supervision, and uncertainty~estimation.
